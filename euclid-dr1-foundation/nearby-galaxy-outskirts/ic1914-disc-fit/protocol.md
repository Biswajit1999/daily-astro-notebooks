# IC 1914 disc model test

Freeze the published IC1914-01 and IC1914-03 locations. Measure the same 5″-radius apertures before and after subtraction of a smooth disc with radius-dependent axis ratio and position angle. The centre is the published S4G centre, the models use concentric ellipses, and neither candidate contributes to the primary profile or shape fit within 12″. Repeat with an 18″ exclusion. This is a follow-up of VIS-selected positions, so the VIS ranks are descriptive.

Use MER background-restored VIS as the primary optical image, Legacy Surveys DR10 r as an independent optical check, and Euclid NISP H for the near infrared. Fit a separate radial light profile and geometry in each band. Compare the fixed S4G shape with a fitted constant ellipse and with a smoothly varying shape. Compare the two fixed positions to the same nonoverlapping original-geometry apertures in all models, using common candidate and control pixels within each band. Keep the excess in each image's own units, and report the fraction of control values below it. Compare fits in held-out angular blocks to assess whether radial freedom improves predictions outside the target apertures.

If the signal remains, inspect its spatial connection to the outer disc. A positive local residual after an axisymmetric subtraction can be produced by a spiral arm as well as tidal material. Require independent morphological or kinematic evidence before claiming a tidal feature. The study will not treat lack of a cross-identification as evidence for novelty.

Secondary check, added after the first elliptical fits showed both excesses. Hold the varying ellipse geometry fixed and fit smooth azimuthal Fourier terms of orders 1 and 2 outside the same candidate exclusions. Use coarse radial knots so the fit accounts for broad disc asymmetry and cannot put a narrow feature at a chosen position. Assess both candidate changes and angular holdout errors. The Fourier check is exploratory.
