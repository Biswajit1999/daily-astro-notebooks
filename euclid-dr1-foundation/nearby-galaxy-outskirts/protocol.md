# Search protocol, 16 November 2026

Question: can a pilot search of nearby S4G galaxies in Euclid DR1-Foundation find extended off-disc optical structures worth independent follow-up?

Sample fixed before the images are opened. Use the IRSA S4G catalogue and Wide Survey MER catalogue MOC. Require S4G 3.6 micron 25.5 mag arcsec^-2 semimajor axis 60–120 arcsec, axis ratio no larger than 0.60, finite catalogue distance <=25 Mpc, and a catalogue centre in the MOC. Rank by decreasing semimajor axis; use the first eight, with no replacements after looking at imagery. Record coverage failures explicitly. The intended search is near the host on scales of about 40–105 arcsec, outside 1.15 times the S4G isophotal ellipse, in each valid VIS stamp.

Screen the observations for extended positive residuals with robust azimuthal baseline subtraction and two spatial scales. Look at the raw science map as well as the residuals. Treat replicated morphology in the independent NISP detector, a separate VIS observation or archival ground-based exposures as a check on astrophysical reality. A rotated local control and one-band artifacts remain plausible alternatives. Distinguish foreground cirrus, diffuse host light, a known feature, and a previously unreported candidate. Lack of an archive match does not establish novelty. Exclude candidates from physical interpretation when coverage, source masks, foreground, or uncertainty is unresolved.

The pilot is exploratory and small. Keep nulls, publish the observed sample/selection and image checks, and make no completeness or discovery claim without additional evidence.
