# Looking for wide binary star pairs near Alpha Centauri

A wide binary is a pair of stars far enough apart to look like two separate points in the sky, but still
gravitationally bound to each other. The way to tell a real pair from two unrelated stars that just happen
to line up is that true binaries share nearly identical parallax (distance) and proper motion (sky motion).
I searched real Gaia data around the Alpha Centauri system — our nearest stellar neighbors — for stars
sharing its parallax and motion, as a simple common-motion check.

I queried a two-degree field around Alpha Centauri's position with a high parallax cut (since the system is
extremely close, at about 750 milliarcseconds of parallax), then plotted every matching star's proper motion
colored by its parallax.

The stars that share both the very high parallax and closely matching proper motion in this small patch of
sky are the Alpha Centauri system's own components. Seeing them fall directly out of a plain parallax and
proper-motion query, without needing to already know which points on the sky "were" the stars I was looking
for, made the logic behind wide-binary catalogs click for me in a way that reading about the method hadn't.

**What I'd look at next:** widen the search and loosen the parallax cut to see whether any less famous
common-motion companions turn up further from the main system.
