# The Hyades cluster: an HR diagram for the nearest open cluster to us

The Hyades is the closest open star cluster to the Sun, about 47 parsecs (roughly 153 light-years) away,
which makes it an excellent benchmark since Gaia can measure its stars extremely precisely. I queried real
Gaia data for stars in its direction, applied a parallax cut around the cluster's known distance, and built
an HR diagram (brightness vs. color) the same way I did for the Pleiades.

I also printed the median measured distance of my candidate members as a sanity check against the cluster's
textbook distance, which came out very close.

The Hyades main sequence looked a touch shorter at the bright end compared to the Pleiades diagram I made
earlier — consistent with the Hyades being noticeably older (around 600-700 million years). Its most
massive stars have already had time to burn through their core hydrogen and start evolving away from the
main sequence, which is exactly why astronomers use where the main sequence "turns off" as a way to
estimate a cluster's age.

The nice part about this pair of notebooks is that I didn't have to take anyone's word for the age
difference between these two clusters — it's directly visible in the shape of the main sequence built from
real data in each case.
