# An HR diagram for NGC 188, one of the oldest nearby open clusters

NGC 188 is an open star cluster that's unusually old for its type — roughly 6-7 billion years, far older
than the Pleiades or Hyades I looked at earlier in this folder — which makes it a good target for seeing how
an HR diagram (brightness plotted against color) changes shape as a cluster ages. I built one from real Gaia
data using a parallax cut around the cluster's known distance.

The main sequence in this cluster's HR diagram cuts off noticeably lower (fainter and cooler) than in the
Pleiades or Hyades diagrams, and there's a visible clump of redder, brighter stars sitting above the main
sequence — red giants, meaning stars that have already burned through their core hydrogen and puffed up into
a later evolutionary stage.

That combination — a low main-sequence turn-off point plus a populated giant branch — is exactly the
signature astronomers look for to identify an old cluster, and seeing it emerge from real photometry rather
than a diagram in a textbook made the age-dating logic much more concrete for me.

**What I'd look at next:** compare the turn-off point against theoretical stellar evolution model tracks
(isochrones) to get an actual quantitative age estimate for the cluster, rather than just "older" or
"younger" by eye.
