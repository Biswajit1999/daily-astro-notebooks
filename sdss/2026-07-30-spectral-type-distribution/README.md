# What kinds of objects has SDSS classified in one small patch of sky?

Instead of looking at a single object, I queried a 2x2 degree patch of sky (RA 150-152, Dec 1-3) and asked
SDSS's own spectroscopic pipeline what it had classified there — stars, galaxies, or quasars — to see the
mix.

This turned into a nice check on how survey targeting actually works. SDSS doesn't take spectra of a random
sample of everything in the sky; it deliberately selects targets according to specific programs (a
magnitude-limited galaxy survey, separate stellar and quasar target lists, and so on), so whatever mix shows
up in any given patch reflects those targeting choices more than the "true" underlying population.

Galaxies dominated the counts by a wide margin in this particular patch, which lines up with SDSS's main
spectroscopic sample having been built around a large galaxy survey. I also pulled the redshift ranges for
the galaxies and quasars found here, just to get a feel for how much farther the quasars reach compared to
the galaxies.

What surprised me a little was just how few quasars showed up relative to galaxies and stars — a reminder
that even though quasars are individually extremely luminous, they're intrinsically rare compared to
ordinary galaxies, so they need much more sky and much fainter magnitude limits to show up in large numbers.
