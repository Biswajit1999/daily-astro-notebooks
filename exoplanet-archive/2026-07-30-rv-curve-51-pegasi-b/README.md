# 51 Pegasi b: the first exoplanet found around a sun-like star

51 Pegasi b was the first exoplanet ever confirmed orbiting a normal, sun-like star, discovered in 1995
using the radial velocity method — watching a star's own light wobble in wavelength as an unseen planet's
gravity tugs it back and forth. I pulled its real, current best-fit orbital parameters from the NASA
Exoplanet Archive and used them to reconstruct what that radial velocity wobble looks like over one full
orbit.

Using the archive's reported orbital period and radial velocity semi-amplitude, I built a simple model curve
of the star's velocity over time (the orbit is nearly circular, so a simple cosine curve is a good
approximation).

The period is famously short — about 4.2 days, meaning this planet orbits closer to its star than Mercury
orbits our Sun. That's exactly why the discovery was such a surprise in 1995: nobody expected a giant planet
to exist in an orbit that tight, since nothing in our own solar system looks like that. The velocity wobble
itself is tiny, tens of meters per second (roughly a brisk walking pace), which is a good reminder of how
precise the spectroscopy behind this measurement had to be, even thirty years ago.

**What I'd look at next:** pull the actual published radial velocity data points from the original discovery
paper and overlay them against this idealized model curve.
