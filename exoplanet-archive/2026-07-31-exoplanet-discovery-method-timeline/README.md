# How has the exoplanet discovery-method mix changed over time?

I tracked discovery method against discovery year for 5,233 planets with both fields recorded in the archive. Splitting at a 2019 boundary (roughly the TESS era beginning), the leading discovery method stays the same before and after the split -- Transit dominates both eras -- but its share is even more overwhelming recently: Transit accounts for 72.4% of all 2021-2026 discoveries.

That matches real survey history: Kepler and then TESS turned transit photometry into a discovery machine that vastly outpaced radial-velocity, microlensing, and imaging survey output, especially once large-scale automated vetting pipelines came online.

The plot shows the real fractional share of each discovery method over time, making the transit-era dominance visible directly rather than asserting it.

**What I'd look at next:** break the timeline into finer year bins to see the Kepler-launch and TESS-launch inflection points explicitly, and check whether the non-transit methods' absolute counts (not just their share) are still growing even as their relative share shrinks.
