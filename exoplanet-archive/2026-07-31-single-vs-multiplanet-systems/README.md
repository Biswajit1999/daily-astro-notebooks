# Are planets in multi-planet systems smaller?

I compared planet radii between single-transiting-planet hosts and multi-transiting-planet hosts using a quality-cut sample of 4,646 planets from the archive.

The result is a real, negative contrast: planets in multi-planet systems have a median radius 0.62 Earth radii smaller than planets in single-planet systems (bootstrap 95% interval -0.72 to -0.50 Earth radii), confirming that compact multi-planet systems tend to host smaller, more uniformly sized planets -- a pattern noted in prior Kepler-based multiplicity studies. Restricting to planets discovered before 2023, the contrast is similar though slightly smaller in magnitude (-0.51 Earth radii), suggesting the effect isn't purely a product of recent, smaller-planet-biased TESS discoveries.

The plot compares the radius distributions of the two groups directly.

**What I'd look at next:** control for orbital period and detection-sensitivity differences between single- and multi-planet-flagged systems, since a system's flagged multiplicity itself depends partly on how many of its planets happened to transit and be detectable.

**Citation:** NASA Exoplanet Archive, Caltech/NASA Exoplanet Exploration Program: https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/counts_detail.html
