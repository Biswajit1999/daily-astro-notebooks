# HD 189733 b: a hot Jupiter with one of the best-studied atmospheres

HD 189733 b is a "hot Jupiter" — a gas giant crammed into a scorchingly close orbit around its star — and
it's one of the most intensively studied exoplanets in existence, mostly because it's both bright and
nearby, making it a favorite target for atmosphere studies from space telescopes. I pulled its real orbital
and physical parameters from the NASA Exoplanet Archive to see just how extreme its environment really is,
and reconstructed its radial velocity wobble the same way I did for 51 Pegasi b.

With a roughly 2.2-day orbital period and an equilibrium temperature reported well over 1000 Kelvin, this
planet is genuinely baking. Real observations (not part of this notebook, but well known in the literature)
have found evidence of fast winds circulating material around the planet, blurring the difference between
its permanent day and night sides.

The radial velocity signal this planet produces is tens of meters per second — much easier to detect than a
small rocky planet would produce, which is part of why hot Jupiters like this one were among the very first
exoplanets ever discovered, well before smaller planets became detectable.

**What I'd look at next:** look into published transmission spectroscopy results for this planet (light
filtered through its atmosphere during transit), since it's one of the best-characterized exoplanet
atmospheres we currently have.
