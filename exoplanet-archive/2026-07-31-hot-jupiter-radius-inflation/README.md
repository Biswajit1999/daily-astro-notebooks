# Are hot Jupiters bigger around hotter stars?

Using a quality-cut sample of 698 close-in giant planets, I tested whether planet radius correlates with host-star effective temperature -- a proxy for stellar irradiation, which several proposed mechanisms predict should inflate a hot Jupiter's atmosphere.

The Spearman correlation comes out positive and significant: rho = 0.558, with a bootstrap 68% interval of 0.528-0.584. Restricting to a mass-matched sub-sample (529 planets, controlling for the fact that more massive planets resist inflation more) the correlation actually strengthens slightly, to rho = 0.617. That's a real, quantified confirmation of the radius-inflation trend, not just a visual impression from a scatter plot.

The plot shows planet radius against host-star temperature with the correlation and its bootstrap uncertainty annotated directly.

**What I'd look at next:** fit an actual inflation model (e.g. the Thorngren & Fortney 2018 heating-efficiency relation) instead of a rank correlation, to get a physically interpretable inflation efficiency rather than just a monotonic trend strength.

**Citation:** NASA Exoplanet Archive, Caltech/NASA Exoplanet Exploration Program: https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/counts_detail.html
