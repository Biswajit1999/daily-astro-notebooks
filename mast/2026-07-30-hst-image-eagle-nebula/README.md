# The Pillars of Creation: a real Hubble image of the Eagle Nebula

The "Pillars of Creation" in the Eagle Nebula is one of the most recognized astronomical images ever taken —
towering columns of gas and dust where new stars are forming. Instead of just admiring the iconic
press-release picture, I queried MAST for real Hubble ACS/WFC observations of the Eagle Nebula and pulled
down an actual combined-filter preview image with a script.

I resolved the nebula's coordinates, searched for ACS/WFC imaging observations nearby, and picked out a
"total" color-combined preview product from the results rather than a single narrow-band frame, so the
final image would show real color contrast between different emission features.

It's a genuinely different feeling pulling this straight from the archive with a query, rather than
downloading a press photo — this is a real combined-filter mosaic product built by the same archive pipeline
that processes every Hubble observation, of the same structures made famous in 1995 and again in Hubble's
sharper 2014 re-observation.

**What I'd look at next:** specifically compare the original 1995 WFPC2 observation of the Pillars against
the 2014 ACS re-observation to see the improvement in resolution and depth directly, rather than only looking
at one epoch.
