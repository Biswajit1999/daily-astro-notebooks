# A real JWST view of the Ring Nebula, a nearby planetary nebula

The Ring Nebula is a "planetary nebula" — a glowing shell of gas blown off by a dying, sun-like star, with
nothing to do with actual planets (the name is a historical accident from how these objects looked in old,
low-resolution telescopes). JWST imaged it in striking detail, and I wanted to pull real NIRCam observations
of it straight from the MAST archive myself, using the same query pattern I used for the Carina Nebula
elsewhere in this folder.

I searched by coordinates for JWST NIRCam imaging observations near the nebula, pulled the product list, and
grabbed the calibrated mosaic preview image to display.

This shell was ejected only a few thousand years ago in astronomical terms and is still visibly expanding.
What makes JWST's infrared view special compared to earlier Hubble images is its sensitivity to the cooler,
more diffuse gas and dust further out from the bright central ring, which shows up much better at infrared
wavelengths than in visible light.

**What I'd look at next:** compare this NIRCam (near-infrared) preview against a MIRI (mid-infrared)
observation of the same nebula, since the two instruments are known to highlight quite different structures
in the surrounding gas.
