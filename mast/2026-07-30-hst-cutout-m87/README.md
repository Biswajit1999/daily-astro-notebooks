# A real Hubble image of M87, the galaxy with the famous black hole jet

M87 is a giant elliptical galaxy at the center of the Virgo Cluster, famous for the relativistic jet of
material blasting out of its central supermassive black hole — the same black hole the Event Horizon
Telescope imaged directly in 2019. I queried MAST for real Hubble WFC3/UVIS observations of M87 and pulled
down an actual preview image with a script to look at the galaxy directly.

I searched by the galaxy's coordinates, pulled the product list for one of the matching observations, and
grabbed a combined "total" preview image rather than a single narrow filter frame.

M87 sits about 55 million light-years away, and its central black hole is one of the most massive known —
billions of times the Sun's mass. Depending on the exact pointing this particular preview landed on, the
jet itself may or may not be clearly visible in this frame, since it's a fairly narrow, specific feature
rather than something that fills an entire general survey image — a good reminder that "an image of a
famous object" and "an image showing the famous feature of that object" aren't always the same thing.

**What I'd look at next:** specifically search MAST for the observation programs that targeted the jet
directly, rather than a general survey pointing, to reliably capture that feature.
